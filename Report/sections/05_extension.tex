\subsection{CP-Decomposition Analysis}

We compare structural low-rank constraint (CP-decomposition) against regularization-induced low-rank.
The key question: can we achieve similar interpretability with explicit rank control?

Unlike dense bilinear layers where low-rank structure emerges from regularization (noise augmentation and weight decay), CP-decomposition explicitly constrains the interaction tensor to rank $R$ through the factorization in Equation~\ref{eq:cp}.
This provides direct control over the model's capacity and interpretability trade-off.

\subsubsection{Rank Sweep}

We train CP-decomposed bilinear models with ranks $R \in \{8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256\}$ on MNIST, using minimal regularization (weight decay $0.1$, no noise augmentation).
Table~\ref{tab:cp_sweep} reports accuracy and effective rank for each configuration, compared to Phase 1 dense baselines.

\textbf{Key Findings:}

First, effective rank does not scale linearly with CP rank.
While $R=32$ achieves effective rank $11.7$, $R=64$ achieves a lower effective rank of $3.5$ despite higher capacity.
This suggests that the model learns to concentrate information in fewer components when given sufficient capacity, consistent with the low-rank interpretability hypothesis.

Second, CP-decomposition achieves comparable or better interpretability than dense models without noise augmentation.
CP $R=32$ achieves effective rank $11.7$, significantly lower than dense (full reg) at $36.30$, while maintaining $93.2\%$ accuracy (vs $98.30\%$ for dense full reg).
This demonstrates that structural low-rank constraint can replace regularization-induced low-rank for interpretability, with the advantage of explicit rank control.

Third, the optimal accuracy-interpretability trade-off occurs at $R=64$, achieving $91.2\%$ accuracy with effective rank $3.5$.
This represents a $10\times$ reduction in effective rank compared to dense (no reg) while maintaining reasonable accuracy.

\subsection{Structure vs. Regularization: Pareto Analysis}

Comparing structural (CP) and regularization-induced low-rank reveals a fundamental trade-off.
Dense models with full regularization achieve higher accuracy ($98.30\%$) but require noise augmentation, which increases training complexity and may not generalize to all domains.
CP models achieve lower effective rank ($3.5$ for $R=64$ vs $36.30$ for dense full reg) without noise augmentation, but sacrifice some accuracy ($91.2\%$ vs $98.30\%$).

The key advantage of CP-decomposition is explicit rank control: we can directly set the interaction tensor rank $R$ rather than relying on regularization hyperparameters to induce low-rank structure.
This makes CP more suitable for applications requiring guaranteed interpretability bounds or when noise augmentation is undesirable.

\subsection{Summary}

Our extension experiments address three key questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Is the low-rank interpretable structure robust to distribution shift?}
    Robustness analysis on rotated MNIST and EMNIST is pending (experiments in progress).
    
    \item \textbf{Can structural constraints (CP) replace regularization for interpretability?}
    Yes. CP-decomposition achieves lower effective rank than dense (full reg) models without noise augmentation.
    CP $R=32$ achieves effective rank $11.7$ (vs $36.30$ for dense full reg), demonstrating that explicit rank constraints can replace regularization-induced low-rank structure.
    The trade-off is reduced accuracy ($93.2\%$ vs $98.30\%$), but CP provides direct control over the interpretability-accuracy frontier.
    
    \item \textbf{What is the accuracy-interpretability Pareto frontier?}
    Based on preliminary single-seed results, CP $R=64$ achieves the best trade-off: $91.2\%$ accuracy with effective rank $3.5$.
    Full multi-seed analysis will refine this frontier and provide statistical significance.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations:} Current results are based on single-seed experiments (seed 42).
Multi-seed runs (seeds 42--46) are in progress and will provide standard deviations and statistical significance tests.
The Pareto frontier analysis will be updated once multi-seed results are available.
