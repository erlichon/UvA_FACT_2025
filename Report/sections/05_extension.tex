\section{Extension Results}
\label{sec:extension}

Beyond reproducing the original findings, we extend the analysis in two directions:
(1) robustness of interpretable structure under distribution shift (Extension 2), and
(2) comparing structural low-rank (CP-decomposition) against regularization-induced low-rank (Extension 3).

%==============================================================================
\subsection{Extension 2: Cross-Dataset Robustness}
\label{sec:extension2}
%==============================================================================

The paper demonstrates that regularized bilinear MLPs learn interpretable eigenvectors on MNIST.
A natural question is whether these learned representations are specific to MNIST's particular digit styles or capture more universal digit structure that transfers across datasets.

\subsubsection{Experimental Setup}

We train bilinear models on both MNIST and EMNIST-Digits using the paper's regularization settings (noise std $= 0.5$, weight decay $= 1.0$) with center-of-mass normalization.
We evaluate transfer in three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Functional transfer}: Does a MNIST-trained model generalize to EMNIST-Digits and USPS?
    \item \textbf{Representational overlap}: Do eigenvectors for the same digit class share subspace structure across models?
    \item \textbf{Shape similarity}: Do visually similar digit-letter pairs (0-O, 1-I) share eigenvector structure?
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Key findings:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MNIST $\leftrightarrow$ EMNIST-Digits}: Strong bidirectional transfer (97.7\% average).
    The MNIST model achieves 97.5\% on EMNIST-Digits (vs.\ 98.2\% native), a drop of only 0.7\%.
    
    \item \textbf{MNIST $\to$ USPS}: The regularized model achieves 72.3\% on USPS, a \textbf{+18.9\%} improvement over the baseline (53.4\%).
    This substantial gap demonstrates that regularization learns more transferable features.
    
    \item \textbf{Per-class analysis on USPS}: Regularization dramatically improves transfer for most digits (e.g., digit 0: 8.6\% $\to$ 78.6\%; digit 8: 16.9\% $\to$ 91.0\%), though some remain challenging (digit 1: 0\% $\to$ 12.1\%).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Extension 3: CP-Decomposition Analysis}
\label{sec:extension3}
%==============================================================================

We compare structural low-rank constraint (CP-decomposition) against regularization-induced low-rank.
The key question: can we achieve similar interpretability with explicit rank control?

\subsubsection{Background}

Unlike dense bilinear layers where low-rank structure emerges from regularization, CP-decomposition explicitly constrains the interaction tensor to rank $R$ through factorization:
\begin{equation}
    B_{cij} = \sum_{r=1}^{R} \lambda_r A_{cr} B_{ir} C_{jr}
\end{equation}
This provides direct control over the model's capacity and interpretability.

\subsubsection{Rank Sweep Results}

We train CP-decomposed bilinear models with ranks $R \in \{8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256\}$ on MNIST using minimal regularization (weight decay 0.1, no noise).

\begin{table}[h]
    \caption{CP-decomposition rank sweep (seed 42) vs.\ dense baselines. CP achieves lower effective rank than dense models at the cost of some accuracy.}
    \label{tab:cp_sweep}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lcc}
        \toprule
        Model & Accuracy (\%) & Eff.\ Rank \\
        \midrule
        \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textit{Dense Baselines}} \\
        \midrule
        Dense (no reg) & $97.49 \pm 0.05$ & $38.5 \pm 0.7$ \\
        Dense (WD only) & $97.49 \pm 0.05$ & $15.5 \pm 0.3$ \\
        Dense (full reg) & $96.20 \pm 4.0$ & $42.2 \pm 36.1$ \\
        \midrule
        \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textit{CP-Decomposition}} \\
        \midrule
        CP $R=8$ & 18.8 & 2.6 \\
        CP $R=16$ & 93.4 & 8.7 \\
        CP $R=32$ & 93.2 & 11.7 \\
        CP $R=64$ & 91.2 & 3.5 \\
        CP $R=128$ & 91.6 & 3.7 \\
        CP $R=256$ & 91.8 & 3.8 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key findings:}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Effective rank does not scale linearly with CP rank.}
    While $R=32$ achieves effective rank 11.7, increasing to $R=64$ \emph{decreases} effective rank to 3.5.
    The model learns to concentrate information in fewer components when given sufficient capacity.
    
    \item \textbf{CP achieves lower effective rank than dense models.}
    CP $R=64$ achieves effective rank 3.5---a $\mathbf{4.4\times}$ reduction compared to dense (WD only) at 15.5, and $\mathbf{11\times}$ reduction compared to dense (no reg) at 38.5.
    
    \item \textbf{Accuracy-interpretability trade-off.}
    CP models sacrifice accuracy for interpretability: $R=64$ achieves 91.2\% (vs.\ 97.5\% for dense WD).
    However, this 6\% accuracy gap may be acceptable when guaranteed low-rank is required.
    
    \item \textbf{Optimal configuration.}
    $R=64$ achieves the best trade-off: 91.2\% accuracy with effective rank 3.5.
    This represents interpretability comparable to having only $\sim$4 dominant eigenvectors per class.
\end{enumerate}

    \item CP is preferable when explicit rank bounds are required or noise augmentation is undesirable.
\end{itemize}

%==============================================================================
\subsection{Summary}
%==============================================================================

Our extensions reveal:

\textbf{Extension 2 (Cross-Dataset Robustness):}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Strong functional transfer: MNIST models achieve 97.5\% on EMNIST-Digits and 72.3\% on USPS (+18.9\% vs.\ baseline).
    \item Moderate representational overlap: Eigenvector subspaces share structure across datasets (mean cosine 0.39 vs.\ random 0.22).
    \item Class-specificity: Models learn discriminative features, not shape-generic detectors---letters are classified as corresponding digits despite different eigenvector structure.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Extension 3 (CP-Decomposition):}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item CP achieves $4.4\times$ lower effective rank than dense (WD) with 6\% accuracy trade-off.
    \item Effective rank does not scale linearly with CP rank---the model self-organizes to low-rank.
    \item CP provides explicit rank control, useful when guaranteed interpretability bounds are required.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations:}
CP results are single-seed (seed 42); multi-seed runs would provide statistical confidence.
The USPS transfer gap (72.3\% vs.\ native $\sim$95\%) indicates room for improvement in cross-domain generalization.
