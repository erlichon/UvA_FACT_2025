\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What was Easy}

\textbf{Vision experiments were straightforward.}
The bilinear architecture is simple (element-wise multiplication of projections), MNIST trains in $\sim$3 minutes per run, and results were highly consistent (std $<$1\% across 40 runs).
The eigendecomposition methodology is robust---computing $784 \times 784$ decompositions completes in seconds.
The paper's codebase provided clear reference implementations.

\subsection{What was Difficult}

\textbf{Hyperparameter disambiguation:} Code defaults (noise 0.4, WD 0.5) differ from Appendix G.1 values (noise 0.5, WD 1.0).
We used appendix values after verifying they match paper figures.

\textbf{SAE availability:} The paper's \texttt{ts-medium} (``ts-tiny'' in paper) lacks \texttt{mlp-in} SAEs at layer 4 on HuggingFace, requiring pivot to \texttt{fw-medium} for Figure 8 reproduction.

\textbf{Memory constraints:} Interaction matrix computation requires $\mathcal{O}(n_{\text{features}}^2)$ memory.
At expansion 8$\times$ with $d=1024$, this exceeds 100GB.
We implemented streaming computation for consumer hardware.

\textbf{Tracing discrepancy:} Determining whether low correlation reflected bugs or limitations required systematic comparison of five SAE checkpoints.

\subsection{Environmental Impact}
\label{sec:environmental}

All experiments were tracked using \texttt{codecarbon} (Table~\ref{tab:environmental}, Appendix~\ref{app:environmental}).
Total footprint: 0.067 kg CO2 ($\approx$0.4 km driving) across 203 runs and 2.04 wall-clock hours.
Low-rank bilinear models offer sustainable alternatives to scaling dense architectures.

\subsection{Connection to FACT-AI Transparency}

\textbf{Weight-based vs.\ Post-hoc Interpretability.}
Traditional methods (saliency maps, SHAP) explain predictions after the fact.
Bilinear MLPs offer intrinsic transparency: eigenvectors \emph{are} the learned features---no auxiliary models needed.

\textbf{Reproducibility as Transparency.}
Our partial language reproduction highlights an underappreciated dimension: the paper's claims depend on SAE quality, which depends on training duration.
We recommend future work explicitly document SAE training budgets.

\textbf{Accuracy-Interpretability Trade-off.}
Weight decay reduces effective rank from 38.5 to 15.5 ($2.5\times$ interpretability improvement) at $<$1\% accuracy cost---a favorable trade-off for transparent AI deployment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Vision (Section 4): Fully Reproduced.}
Weight decay reduces effective rank from 38.5 to 15.5; eigenvectors show interpretable digit patterns; accuracy remains 97--98\%; results robust across 5 seeds and generalize to Fashion-MNIST.

\textbf{Language (Section 5): Partially Reproduced.}
AND-gate circuit confirmed with clear semantic clusters (positive vs.\ negative concepts).
Low-rank structure is \textbf{Reproducible in Principle, Dependent on Artifact Quality}: correlation improves 2.8$\times$ with SAE training (0.15 $\to$ 0.42), but even the longest-trained public SAEs reach only 32\% vs.\ the paper's 69\% threshold.

\textbf{Key Insight.}
The bilinear decomposition methodology is sound and reproducible.
Low-rank approximation quality depends critically on SAE convergence---practitioners should verify SAE quality before drawing conclusions.

\textbf{Extensions.}
(1) Cross-dataset robustness: regularized models learn transferable geometric circuits (0--O similarity confirmed via Quadratic Form Similarity).
(2) CP-decomposition: achieves 93.8\% accuracy with disentangled, atomic factors at eff.\ rank 17.5.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Eigenvector interpretability is qualitative.
Could not reproduce paper's exact Figure 8 (ts-tiny, layer 4) due to missing \texttt{mlp-in} SAEs.
Public SAEs appear undertrained compared to paper's internal artifacts.

\textbf{Future Work.}
Train SAEs with longer duration to test if 69\% threshold is achievable.
Develop quantitative interpretability metrics for eigenvector quality.
Extend to larger vision models (ViTs).
