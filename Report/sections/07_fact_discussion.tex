\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Practical Challenges}

Vision experiments were straightforward: the bilinear architecture is simple, MNIST trains in $\sim$3 minutes per run, and results were highly consistent (std $<$1\% across 40 runs).
Challenges included hyperparameter disambiguation (code defaults differ from appendix values), SAE availability gaps (requiring pivot to \texttt{fw-medium} for Figure 8, and using 8x instead of paper's 4x expansion at layer 10--11), and memory constraints requiring streaming computation for large interaction matrices.
Additionally, key definitions were ambiguous: the paper does not specify which correlation measure to use (Pearson, Spearman, cosine) or the threshold for ``active'' features; we chose Pearson correlation and active $= z > 0$.
The paper's claim that ``most features'' achieve high rank-2 correlation is not quantified-there is no measure or threshold provided.
We interpret ``most features'' as a median threshold ($>$50\% of features achieving $>$0.75 correlation): under this interpretation, fw-small (65\%) reproduces the claim, while ts-medium (32\%) and fw-medium (45\%) fall short.
Determining whether low correlation reflected bugs or limitations required systematic comparison of five SAE checkpoints.

\subsection{Environmental Impact}
\label{sec:environmental}

All experiments were tracked using \texttt{codecarbon} (Table~\ref{tab:environmental}, Appendix~\ref{app:environmental}).
Total footprint: 0.168 kg CO2 ($\approx$1 km driving) across 204 runs and 20.95 wall-clock hours.
Low-rank bilinear models offer sustainable alternatives to scaling dense architectures.

\subsection{Connection to FACT-AI Transparency}

Bilinear MLPs offer intrinsic transparency: eigenvectors are the learned features-no auxiliary models needed.
Our partial language reproduction highlights that low-rank approximation quality depends on both SAE training duration and model training compute (tokens/parameter): models with higher tokens/param show stronger low-rank behavior.
This emphasizes the importance of documenting training budgets in interpretability research.
Following the tutorial methodology, we identify features 3834 (``not-good'') and 751 (``not-bad'') as semantic opposites with weakly negative cosine similarity ($-0.16$) in SAE decoder space-what the tutorial calls a ``somewhat linear subspace.''
As additional validation beyond the paper, we test the same circuit on both TinyStories and FineWeb-16k: correlations remain consistent (0.93/0.83 vs.\ 0.91/0.82), demonstrating robustness to distribution shift.
Weight decay reduces effective rank from 38.5 to 15.5 ($2.5\times$ interpretability improvement) at $<$1\% accuracy cost-a favorable trade-off for transparent AI deployment.

\subsection{Communication with Original Authors}

We did not communicate with the original authors during this reproduction study.
All experiments were conducted using the publicly available code repository and pretrained model artifacts released alongside the paper.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We fully reproduce vision results: weight decay reduces effective rank from 38.5 to 15.5 while maintaining 97--98\% accuracy with interpretable digit patterns.
Language results are partially reproduced: AND-gate circuits confirmed with semantic opposites (not-good vs.\ not-bad, cosine similarity $-0.16$), but the fraction of features with high rank-2 correlation varies by model (32\%--65\%).
Under our median interpretation ($>$50\% threshold), fw-small (65\%) reproduces the ``most features'' claim, while ts-medium (32\%) and fw-medium (45\%) fall short.
The bilinear decomposition methodology is sound; low-rank approximation quality depends on both SAE convergence and model training compute (tokens/parameter).
Extensions demonstrate transferable geometric circuits across datasets and CP-decomposition achieving 93.8\% accuracy with disentangled factors at eff.\ rank 17.5, while gaining significant efficiency (30 times faster training).
Future work should develop quantitative interpretability metrics and extend to larger vision models.
