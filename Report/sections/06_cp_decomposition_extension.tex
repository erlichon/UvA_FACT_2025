\section{CP-Decomposition Analysis}
\label{sec:cp_extension}

We extend the Bilinear MLP framework by proposing Canonical Polyadic (CP) decomposition as an \emph{architectural constraint} during training, shifting from discovering low-rank structure post-hoc to enforcing intrinsic interpretability.

\subsection{Motivation: From Post-Hoc to Intrinsic Interpretability}

Standard eigendecomposition enforces orthogonality, creating \emph{entangled} features---mixed concepts artificially separated to satisfy mathematical constraints.
For example, vertical and horizontal strokes in digits ``4'' and ``7'' must be forced into mutually exclusive eigenvectors.

CP-decomposition parameterizes the interaction tensor directly as a sum of rank-1 tensors:
\begin{equation}
    W_{cij} = \sum_{r=1}^{R} \lambda_r A_{cr} B_{ir} C_{jr}
    \label{eq:cp_factorization}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the maximum rank and $\lambda_r$ are learnable scaling factors~\citep{veeramacheneni2022canonical}.
This factorization does \emph{not} enforce orthogonality, enabling \emph{disentangled} representations where factors naturally overlap.

\subsection{Methodology: Regularization Variants}

We investigate three CP variants:
\textbf{Fixed CP:} Hard rank constraint with no additional regularization---strict complexity bound.
\textbf{Lambda CP:} $L_1$ regularization on learnable scales $\lambda_r$ for soft sparsity~\citep{veeramacheneni2022canonical}.
\textbf{Gated CP:} Stochastic gates with $L_0$ proxy for explicit rank pruning~\citep{cao2024learning}.
All variants trained on MNIST with weight decay 0.1 across rank sweep $R \in \{8, \ldots, 784\}$.

\textbf{Two distinct regimes:}
(1) \textbf{Fixed CP} operates as a strict bottleneck with eff.\ rank $\sim$3.5 regardless of allocated capacity, at $\sim$6\% accuracy cost.
(2) \textbf{Lambda/Gated CP} at $R=256$ achieve \textbf{93.8\% accuracy with eff.\ rank 17.5}---matching dense model complexity while providing structural disentanglement.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis: Disentangled Factors}

Figure~\ref{fig:cp_comparison} (Appendix~\ref{app:cp_full}) compares eigenvectors from Dense vs.\ CP models.
Dense eigenvectors resemble superimposed digit templates---entangled representations combining strokes for orthogonality.
CP factors appear more \textbf{atomic} and \textbf{localized}: top loops, vertical strokes, bottom curves that can be linearly combined.
This aligns with ``parts-based'' representations sought in interpretability.

\subsection{Conclusion}

CP-decomposition serves dual purposes:
(1) \textbf{Intrinsic interpretability} via disentangled, atomic parts rather than entangled templates.
(2) \textbf{Scalable efficiency} by reducing parameters from $\mathcal{O}(d_{\text{hidden}} \times d_{\text{in}}^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(R \times d_{\text{in}})$.
Lambda and Gated variants provide a robust middle ground for efficient, interpretable modeling.
