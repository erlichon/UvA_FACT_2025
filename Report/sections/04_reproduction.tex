\section{Reproduction Results}
\label{sec:reproduction}

This section presents our reproduction of the main experiments from \citet{pearce2024bilinear}: Section 4 (Vision) on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST and Section 5 (Language) on bilinear transformers.

\subsection{Vision Results (Paper Section 4)}
\label{sec:vision_results}

We trained bilinear MLPs on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST under four regularization configurations across 5 random seeds each.

\subsubsection{Main Result: Regularization Induces Low-Rank Structure}

The paper's central claim is that regularization induces low-rank structure in the bilinear interaction tensor.
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_combined} confirms this through our ablation experiments.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/vision/mnist_ablation.pdf}
        \caption{MNIST: Accuracy vs.\ effective rank}
        \label{fig:mnist_ablation}
    \end{subfigure}
    \hfill
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/vision/fashion_ablation.pdf}
        \caption{Fashion-MNIST: Accuracy vs.\ effective rank}
        \label{fig:fashion_ablation}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Ablation study showing accuracy-interpretability trade-off across regularization configurations.
    Weight decay achieves the lowest effective rank on both datasets while maintaining competitive accuracy.
    The effective rank ratio (regularized/unregularized) meets the paper's $<0.5$ criterion.}
    \label{fig:ablation_combined}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Key findings:}
(1) \textbf{Weight decay is the primary driver of low-rank structure}---achieving even lower effective rank than the fully regularized model.
(2) \textbf{Noise augmentation increases effective rank} while improving accuracy, spreading variance across more eigenvectors.
(3) The effective rank ratio (15.5/38.5 $\approx$ 0.40) meets the paper's $<0.5$ criterion, confirming Claim V1.

\subsection{Language Results (Paper Section 5)}
\label{sec:language_results}

We reproduce the negation circuit discovery using pretrained bilinear transformers and publicly available SAEs from HuggingFace.\footnote{SAE repositories: \texttt{tdooms/fw-medium-scope}, \texttt{tdooms/ts-medium-scope}, \texttt{tdooms/fw-small-scope}.}

\textbf{Critical finding: SAE availability gap.}
Inspection of the public SAE repositories revealed a limitation: \texttt{ts-medium-scope} (the paper's ts-tiny model) only provides \texttt{mlp-in} SAEs for layer 5, not layer 4.
Since Figure 8 analysis requires \emph{both} \texttt{mlp-in} and \texttt{mlp-out} SAEs, the paper's Figure 8 (ts-tiny, layer 4) cannot be reproduced with public artifacts.
We therefore use \texttt{fw-medium} (layer 7, expansion 8) which has both SAE types available.

\subsubsection{Negation Circuit Discovery (Paper Figure 8)}

We reproduce the AND-gate circuit discovery demonstrating how bilinear interactions implement semantic operations.
Using a memory-efficient eigensolver, we systematically searched all 8,192 output features in fw-medium and identified feature 751 as having the strongest AND-gate structure (AND-score 0.96).

Figure~\ref{fig:negation_circuit} compares the tutorial feature 3834 with our discovered feature 751.
Feature 751 exhibits clear block structure in its interaction matrix and separates input features into two semantic clusters: \textbf{positive sentiment} (3 features: ``wonderful'', ``excellent'', ``fortunate'') and \textbf{negative concepts} (12 features: ``horrible'', ``disease'', ``unfair'', etc.).
The dominant eigenvalues have opposing signs ($\lambda_1 = -0.46$, $\lambda_2 = +0.18$), creating the AND-gate behavior.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/language/figure_8_final.pdf}
    \caption{AND-gate circuit comparison (fw-medium, layer 7).
    \textbf{Top}: Feature 3834 (tutorial) shows diffuse structure.
    \textbf{Bottom}: Feature 751 (AND-score 0.96) shows clear block structure with opposing eigenvalues ($\lambda_1 < 0$, $\lambda_2 > 0$).
    Axes in (B) show projection onto top eigenvectors; clusters separate along these directions.}
    \label{fig:negation_circuit}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Evidence:} On test sentences, feature 751 activates for negative-only contexts (``The disease spread quickly'' $\to$ 0.88), moderately when both clusters appear (0.62), and not at all for positive-only (0.0).
This confirms the AND-gate mechanism: the opposing eigenvalues cause cancellation when only one cluster is present.
Full methodology in Appendix~\ref{app:figure8_investigation}.

\subsubsection{Low-Rank Correlation Analysis (Paper Figure 9)}

The paper claims 69\% of features achieve $>$0.75 rank-2 correlation.
Figure~\ref{fig:correlation_analysis} presents our reproduction across three bilinear transformers using configurations closest to those described in the paper.%
\footnote{The paper specifies ``approximately 2/3 model depth'' and 4$\times$ expansion for Figure 9. Available SAEs at \texttt{fw-medium} layer 7 use 8$\times$ expansion.}

    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}

        \caption{Correlation vs.\ rank}
    \end{subfigure}
    \hfill
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}

\textbf{Discrepancy:} Our rank-2 correlations (18--32\% above 0.75) are substantially lower than the paper's 69\%.
We verified our implementation uses the same Pearson correlation metric, computed on active samples only.
The discrepancy persists across all three models, suggesting an artifact quality gap rather than methodological error.

\subsubsection{Root Cause Analysis: SAE Training Duration}

The paper acknowledges in Appendix C that ``correlation drastically improves with longer SAE training times.''
We test this using fw-medium's layer 12 SAEs (expansion=16), where training-time checkpoints v0--v4 are publicly available.%
\footnote{Layer 12 v0--v4 checkpoints are the only training-time variants available. No such variants exist for layer 7 used in Figure~\ref{fig:correlation_analysis}.}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/language/figure_10a_sae_training_effect.pdf}
        \caption{Correlation vs.\ SAE training time}
    \end{subfigure}
    \hfill
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/language/figure_10b_sae_training_histogram.pdf}
        \caption{Distribution shift with training}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Effect of SAE training duration on interpretability (fw-medium layer 12, expansion 16, active features only).
    From v0 to v4: mean rank-2 correlation improves \textbf{2.8$\times$} (0.15 $\to$ 0.42); percentage above 0.75 threshold improves \textbf{2.7$\times$} (12\% $\to$ 32\%).}
    \label{fig:sae_training_effect}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Key findings:}
(1) Rank-2 correlation improves monotonically with SAE training duration (v0: 12\% $\to$ v4: 32\% above 0.75 threshold).
(2) The distribution shifts rightward with training, matching the paper's description.
(3) Even the longest-trained public SAE (v4) reaches only 32\%, far below the paper's 69\% claim.
(4) This confirms the methodology is sound; the reproducibility gap stems from SAE quality, not methodological flaws.

\subsection{Summary}

\textbf{Vision (Section 4): Fully Reproduced.} All claims (V1--V6) confirmed.

\textbf{Language (Section 5): Partially Reproduced.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt,itemsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{AND-gate circuits (L1--L2): Reproduced.} We successfully discover circuits with clear semantic structure. Feature 751 shows stronger AND-gate properties than the tutorial example.
    \item \textbf{Low-rank correlation (L3): Not reproduced with public artifacts.} Public SAEs yield rank-2 correlations of 0.27--0.51, far below the claimed 69\% above 0.75.
    \item \textbf{Root cause: SAE training duration.} Training-time analysis confirms correlation improves with longer SAE training. The paper likely used internal, better-trained SAEs not publicly available.
\end{itemize}
