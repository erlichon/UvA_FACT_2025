\section{Reproduction Results}
\label{sec:reproduction}

This section presents our reproduction of the main experiments from \citet{pearce2024bilinear}: Section 4 (Vision) on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST and Section 5 (Language) on bilinear transformers.

%==============================================================================
\subsection{Vision Results (Paper Section 4)}
\label{sec:vision_results}
%==============================================================================

We trained bilinear MLPs on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST under four regularization configurations across 5 random seeds each, following the paper's experimental setup (Appendix G.1).

\subsubsection{Main Result: Regularization Induces Low-Rank Structure}

The paper's central claim is that regularization (input noise + weight decay) induces low-rank structure in the bilinear interaction tensor.
We verify this through ablation experiments, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_combined}.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/vision/mnist_ablation.pdf}
        \caption{MNIST: Accuracy vs.\ effective rank}
        \label{fig:mnist_ablation}
    \end{subfigure}
    \hfill
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/vision/fashion_ablation.pdf}
        \caption{Fashion-MNIST: Accuracy vs.\ effective rank}
        \label{fig:fashion_ablation}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Ablation study showing the accuracy-interpretability trade-off across four regularization configurations (none, noise-only, weight-decay-only, full).
    Weight decay achieves the lowest effective rank on both datasets while maintaining competitive accuracy.
    The effective rank ratio (regularized / unregularized) meets the paper's $<0.5$ criterion.}
    \label{fig:ablation_combined}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Key findings from the ablation:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Weight decay is the primary driver of low-rank structure.}
    As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_combined}, weight-decay-only achieves the lowest effective rank among all configurations---even lower than the fully regularized model.
    
    \item \textbf{Noise augmentation increases effective rank.}
    Contrary to the paper's emphasis, noise-only achieves \emph{higher} effective rank than the baseline, while improving accuracy.
    Noise prevents overfitting but spreads variance across more eigenvectors.
    
    \item \textbf{The effective rank ratio meets the paper's criterion.}
    The ratio of regularized to unregularized effective rank is well below 0.5, confirming Claim V1 (Low-Rank Emergence).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Language Results (Paper Section 5)}
\label{sec:language_results}
%==============================================================================

We reproduce the negation circuit discovery experiments using pretrained bilinear transformers and their associated Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) from HuggingFace.

\subsubsection{Model and SAE Configuration}

Due to SAE availability constraints (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), we use \texttt{fw-medium} (335M parameters, layer 7, expansion 8$\times$) instead of the paper's \texttt{ts-medium}.
This model has both \texttt{mlp-in} and \texttt{mlp-out} SAEs available, enabling full Figure 8 reproduction.

\subsubsection{Negation Circuit Discovery (Paper Figure 8)}

The paper identifies a sentiment negation circuit where input features like ``not good'' and ``not bad'' produce opposing output directions.
Figure~\ref{fig:negation_circuit} shows our reproduction.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/language/figure_8_negation_circuit.pdf}
    \caption{Negation circuit visualization (reproduction of Paper Figure 8).
    Left: Interaction submatrix showing which input feature pairs drive the output.
    Right: Top eigenvectors of the interaction matrix, revealing the principal directions that explain the negation mechanism.
    The circuit implements sentiment negation by learning opposing directions for ``not + positive'' vs.\ ``not + negative'' patterns.}
    \label{fig:negation_circuit}
\end{figure}

Using our memory-efficient eigensolver (required due to the large feature space), we identify:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Negation features that differentially activate on ``not + positive words'' vs.\ ``not + negative words''
    \item Opposing decoder directions between these features (negative cosine similarity)
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:negation_comparison} provides additional analysis of the negation features.

\textbf{Discrepancy:} Our results show substantially lower rank-2 correlations than the paper's claimed 69\% above 0.75.
We investigate this discrepancy in the following section.

\subsubsection{Root Cause Analysis: SAE Training Duration}

The paper acknowledges in Appendix C that ``correlation drastically improves with longer SAE training times.''
We test this hypothesis by comparing five SAE versions (v0--v4), where v4 represents the most trained checkpoint publicly released, analyzing how training duration affects correlation metrics (Figure~\ref{fig:sae_training_effect}).

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/language/figure_10a_sae_training_effect.pdf}
        \caption{Correlation vs.\ SAE training time}
    \end{subfigure}
    \hfill
    \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/language/figure_10b_sae_training_histogram.pdf}
        \caption{Distribution shift with training}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Effect of SAE training duration on interpretability.
    Versions (v0--v4) correspond to increasing training steps provided by the authors.
    (a) Mean rank-2 correlation improves monotonically with training duration, validating the method in principle despite the public artifact gap.
    (b) The correlation distribution shifts rightward with training, though publicly available SAEs do not reach the paper's reported 69\% threshold.}
    \label{fig:sae_training_effect}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Key findings:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Rank-2 correlation improves substantially with SAE training duration
    \item The correlation distribution shifts from bimodal to unimodal, matching the paper's description
    \item Even the most-trained publicly available SAE does not achieve the paper's reported metrics
    \item This suggests the paper used SAEs with longer training than those released on HuggingFace
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of Reproduction Results}
\label{sec:reproduction_summary}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{Vision (Paper Section 4) --- Fully Reproduced:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim V1 (Low-Rank Emergence):} Regularization induces low-rank structure, as shown in Figures~\ref{fig:ablation_combined} and~\ref{fig:eigenspectrum}.
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim V2 (Interpretable Eigenvectors):} Top eigenvectors show digit-like patterns (Figure~\ref{fig:eigenvectors}).
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim V3 (Overfitting Without Regularization):} Unregularized eigenvectors are diffuse (Figure~\ref{fig:eigenvectors_noreg}).
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim V4 (Noise Effect):} Input noise improves eigenvector quality (Figure~\ref{fig:noise_effect}).
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim V5 (Challenge Task):} Eigenvectors recover the target digit (Figure~\ref{fig:challenge}).
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim V6 (Adversarial):} Eigenvector-based perturbations are effective (Figure~\ref{fig:adversarial}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Language (Paper Section 5) --- Partially Reproduced:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Claim L1 (Negation Circuit):} Opposing negation features discovered (Figure~\ref{fig:negation_circuit}).
    \item[$\sim$] \textbf{Claim L2 (Low-Rank Structure):} Reproducible in Principle---correlation improves with SAE quality (Figure~\ref{fig:sae_training_effect}), but public artifacts do not reach paper's threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:correlation_analysis}).
    \item[\checkmark] \textbf{Root Cause Identified:} SAE training duration explains the artifact gap.
\end{itemize}

The vision experiments are fully reproduced.
For language experiments, we classify Claim L2 as \textbf{Reproducible in Principle, Dependent on Artifact Quality}: we validated the core mechanism but could not replicate the specific numerical threshold (69\%) using currently public model weights.
