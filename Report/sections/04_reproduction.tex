\section{Reproduction Results}
\label{sec:reproduction}

This section presents our reproduction of the main experiments from \citet{pearce2024bilinear}: Section 4 (Vision) on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST and Section 5 (Language) on bilinear transformers.

\subsection{Vision Results (Paper Section 4)}
\label{sec:vision_results}

We trained bilinear MLPs on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST under four regularization configurations across 5 random seeds each.

\subsubsection{Main Result: Regularization Induces Low-Rank Structure (Claim V1)}

The paper's central claim is that regularization induces low-rank structure in the bilinear interaction tensor.
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_combined} confirms this through our ablation experiments.

    \caption{Ablation study showing accuracy-interpretability trade-off across regularization configurations.

    \label{fig:ablation_combined}

\textbf{Key findings:}
(1) \textbf{Weight decay is the primary driver of low-rank structure}---achieving even lower effective rank than the fully regularized model (15.5 vs.\ 21.4).
(2) \textbf{Noise augmentation increases effective rank} while improving accuracy, spreading variance across more eigenvectors.
(3) The effective rank ratio (15.5/38.5 $\approx$ 0.40) meets the paper's $<0.5$ criterion, confirming Claim V1.

\subsubsection{Eigenspectrum Analysis}

Eigenvalue distributions reveal sharp spectral decay with regularization (Figure~\ref{fig:eigenspectrum}, Appendix~\ref{app:vision_additional}).
Weight decay produces the steepest spectral decay, concentrating variance in 10--20 eigenvectors.

\subsubsection{Eigenvector Visualization (Claims V2, V3)}

Figures~\ref{fig:eigenvectors_noreg} and~\ref{fig:eigenvectors_reg} (Appendix~\ref{app:vision_additional}) confirm that top eigenvectors are interpretable digit-like patterns under regularization, while unregularized models show diffuse, noise-like patterns.

\subsubsection{Model Size and Truncation (Claim V5)}
\label{sec:model_size_truncation}

We confirm the paper's claim that bilinear MLPs maintain near-full accuracy with a low-rank truncation: truncating to around 20 eigenvectors per class is sufficient even as model size increases.
Figure~\ref{fig:app_truncation} shows similarity and classification error under eigenvector-rank truncation across model sizes.
Top eigenvectors are highly consistent across random seeds, especially for larger models, and truncation error decreases with model size; truncating to around 20 eigenvectors per class is sufficient for near-full accuracy.
In both panels, the shaded envelope denotes a 90\% confidence interval across seeds.

\subsection{Language Results (Paper Section 5)}
\label{sec:language_results}

We reproduce the negation circuit discovery using pretrained bilinear transformers and publicly available SAEs from HuggingFace.\footnote{SAE repositories: \texttt{tdooms/fw-medium-scope}, \texttt{tdooms/ts-medium-scope}, \texttt{tdooms/fw-small-scope}.}

Our correlation analysis follows a consistent experimental protocol: tokenized text batches are passed through each model, we record \texttt{mlp\_in} and \texttt{mlp\_out} activations, encode \texttt{mlp\_out} with the output SAE to obtain sparse feature activations, and compare these to low-rank predictions derived from the interaction eigenpairs. We examine all output SAE features at the specified layer for each model, filtering to \emph{active features}---features with positive activation ($z_{\text{true}} > 0$) for at least one token position. For each active feature, correlations are computed over all available active samples (target-samples = all). Unless otherwise stated, we report Pearson correlation. Datasets follow model training sources: ts-medium uses TinyStories, fw-small and fw-medium use FineWeb-EDU.

\textbf{Critical finding: SAE availability gap.}
Inspection of the public SAE repositories revealed a limitation: \texttt{ts-medium-scope} (the paper's ts-tiny model) only provides \texttt{mlp-in} SAEs for layer 5, not layer 4.
Since Figure 8 analysis requires \emph{both} \texttt{mlp-in} and \texttt{mlp-out} SAEs, the paper's Figure 8 (ts-tiny, layer 4) cannot be reproduced with public artifacts.
We therefore use \texttt{fw-medium} (layer 7, expansion 8) which has both SAE types available.

\subsubsection{Negation Circuit Discovery (Paper Figure 8)}

We reproduce the AND-gate circuit discovery demonstrating how bilinear interactions implement semantic operations.
Using a memory-efficient eigensolver, we systematically searched all 8,192 output features in fw-medium and identified feature 751 as having the strongest AND-gate structure (AND-score 0.96, old).

Feature 751 exhibits clear block structure in its interaction matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:negation_circuit}, Appendix~\ref{app:language_results}) and separates input features into two semantic clusters: \textbf{positive sentiment} (3 features: ``wonderful'', ``excellent'', ``fortunate'') and \textbf{negative concepts} (12 features: ``horrible'', ``disease'', ``unfair'', etc.) (old).
The dominant eigenvalues have opposing signs ($\lambda_1 = -0.46$, $\lambda_2 = +0.18$) (old), creating the AND-gate behavior.

\textbf{Evidence:} On test sentences, feature 751 activates for negative-only contexts (``The disease spread quickly'' $\to$ 0.88), moderately when both clusters appear (0.62), and not at all for positive-only (0.0) (old).
This confirms the AND-gate mechanism: the opposing eigenvalues cause cancellation when only one cluster is present.

\subsubsection{Low-Rank Correlation Analysis (Paper Figure 9)}

The paper claims 69\% of features achieve $>$0.75 rank-2 correlation.
Our reproduction across three bilinear transformers (Figure~\ref{fig:correlation_analysis}, Appendix~\ref{app:language_results}) shows significantly lower values: mean rank-2 correlations range 0.27--0.51, with only 18--32\% exceeding the 0.75 threshold (old).

\textbf{Discrepancy:} We verified our implementation uses the same Pearson correlation metric, computed on active samples only.
The discrepancy persists across all three models, suggesting an artifact quality gap rather than methodological error. Updated correlation results pending rerun.

\subsubsection{Root Cause Analysis: SAE Training Duration}

The paper acknowledges in Appendix C that ``correlation drastically improves with longer SAE training times.''
We test this using fw-medium's layer 12 SAEs (expansion=16), where training-time checkpoints v0--v4 are publicly available (Figure~\ref{fig:sae_training_effect}, Appendix~\ref{app:language_results}).

\textbf{Key findings (old):}
(1) Rank-2 correlation improves monotonically with SAE training duration (v0: 12\% $\to$ v4: 32\% above 0.75 threshold).
(2) The distribution shifts rightward with training, matching the paper's description.
(3) Even the longest-trained public SAE (v4) reaches only 32\%, far below the paper's 69\% claim.
(4) This confirms the methodology is sound; the reproducibility gap stems from SAE quality, not methodological flaws.
Figure~\ref{fig:sae_training_effect} is computed on a smaller FineWeb-EDU sample (163{,}840 tokens; sample size fixed by the run configuration) than the Figure~\ref{fig:correlation_analysis} sweep, which uses substantially more tokens; this is sufficient for the relative training-time comparison but not directly comparable in absolute scale.

\subsection{Summary}

\textbf{Vision (Section 4): Fully Reproduced.} All claims (V1--V6) confirmed.

\textbf{Language (Section 5): Partially Reproduced.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=0pt,itemsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{AND-gate circuits (L1--L2): Reproduced (old).} We successfully discover circuits with clear semantic structure. Feature 751 shows stronger AND-gate properties than the tutorial example.
    \item \textbf{Low-rank correlation (L3): Not reproduced with public artifacts (old).} Public SAEs yield rank-2 correlations of 0.27--0.51, far below the claimed 69\% above 0.75.
    \item \textbf{Root cause: SAE training duration (old).} Training-time analysis confirms correlation improves with longer SAE training. The paper likely used internal, better-trained SAEs not publicly available.
\end{itemize}
