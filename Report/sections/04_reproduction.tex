\subsection{Vision Results (Paper Section 4)}
\label{sec:vision_results}
%==============================================================================

We trained bilinear MLPs on MNIST under four regularization configurations (none, noise-only, weight-decay-only, full) across 5 random seeds each (20 total runs).

    \centering

\subsubsection{Ablation Study}

We disentangle the contributions of noise augmentation and weight decay by training under four configurations.

\begin{table}[h]
    \caption{Ablation study: Effect of regularization components on accuracy and interpretability (MNIST, mean $\pm$ std over 5 seeds)}
    \label{tab:ablation}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
        \toprule
        Configuration & Noise & WD & Accuracy (\%) & Eff. Rank \\
        \midrule
        None & -- & -- & $97.49 \pm 0.12$ & $38.5 \pm 2.1$ \\
        Noise only & 0.4 & -- & $98.41 \pm 0.08$ & $74.8 \pm 3.2$ \\
        WD only & -- & 0.5 & $97.50 \pm 0.15$ & $21.4 \pm 1.8$ \\
        Full & 0.4 & 0.5 & $98.30 \pm 0.10$ & $36.3 \pm 2.5$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Weight decay alone achieves the lowest effective rank (21.4), producing the most low-rank representation.
Noise augmentation improves accuracy but increases effective rank.
The combination balances accuracy and effective rank.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/mnist_ablation.pdf}
    \caption{Ablation study visualization for MNIST showing accuracy and effective rank across regularization configurations.
    Weight decay (WD) achieves the lowest effective rank while maintaining comparable accuracy.}
    \label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure}

We also replicated these experiments on Fashion-MNIST (\cref{tab:fashion_ablation}), observing consistent trends: weight decay produces the lowest effective rank across both datasets.

\subsubsection{Comparison with Paper}

\begin{table}[h]
    \caption{Vision results: Comparison with original paper (Section 4)}
    \label{tab:vision_comparison}
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lccc}
        \toprule
        Metric & Paper & Ours & Match? \\
        \midrule
        Accuracy (no reg) & $\sim$97--98\% & 97.5\% & \checkmark \\
        Accuracy (full reg) & $\sim$94--95\% & 98.3\% & $\sim$ \\
        Eff. Rank (no reg) & $\sim$150--200 & 38.5 & $\times$ \\
        Eff. Rank (full reg) & $\sim$20--40 & 36.3 & \checkmark \\
        Rank Ratio (WD/none) & $<$0.5 & 0.56 & $\sim$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Our effective rank for unregularized models is significantly lower than reported in the paper ($\sim$38 vs $\sim$150--200).
This may be due to differences in initialization, training dynamics, or our use of the ratio-based effective rank formula.
However, the qualitative trend holds: regularization reduces effective rank.

\subsection{Summary}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{Vision (Section 4):}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Claim V1 (Low-Rank Emergence): \textbf{Partially Supported} -- Weight decay reduces effective rank (38.5 $\to$ 21.4), but our baseline ranks are lower than reported in the paper.
    \item Claim V2 (Interpretable Eigenvectors): \textbf{Partially Supported} -- Regularized eigenvectors show more structure, but visual interpretation remains subjective.
    \item Claim V3 (Overfitting Without Reg): \textbf{Not Supported} -- Unregularized models achieve high test accuracy (97.5\%) with no overfitting observed.
    \item Claim V4 (Noise Importance): \textbf{Nuanced} -- Noise improves accuracy but \emph{increases} effective rank, contrary to intuition.
    \item Claim V5 (Cross-Seed Consistency): \textbf{Supported} -- Low variance across 5 seeds for all metrics.
    \item Claim V6 (Adversarial Generation): Not tested in this reproduction.
\end{itemize}
