\section{Cross-Dataset Structural Robustness}
\label{sec:cross_dataset_robustness}

A core promise of weight-based interpretability is that models learn meaningful geometric features rather than memorizing dataset-specific pixel correlations.
We test whether bilinear MLPs learn \textbf{Platonic forms}---e.g., ``0-ness'' as circularity---rather than idiosyncratic MNIST stroke patterns.

Throughout this section, \textbf{baseline} refers to models trained without regularization (noise std = 0, weight decay = 0), while \textbf{regularized} refers to models trained with full regularization (noise std = 0.5, weight decay = 1.0), as specified in Table~\ref{tab:reg_configs}.

All models use \textbf{Center-of-Mass (CoM) normalization}: images are translated so intensity-weighted centroids align, forcing the bilinear layer to focus on \textbf{shape geometry} rather than absolute position.
We probe robustness under three distributional shifts:
(1) \textbf{Writer independence}: MNIST $\leftrightarrow$ EMNIST-Digits (same labels, different writers);
(2) \textbf{Domain transfer}: MNIST $\rightarrow$ USPS (different resolution/collection);
(3) \textbf{Geometric semantics}: MNIST $\rightarrow$ EMNIST-Letters, where letters are semantically different but geometrically similar (O$\to$0, I$\to$1, Z$\to$2, S$\to$5).

\subsection{Functional Analysis: Regularization Enables Transfer}

\subsubsection{Writer Independence: MNIST $\leftrightarrow$ EMNIST-Digits}

With the regularized CoM model, cross-dataset accuracy is near-perfect and symmetric (Table~\ref{tab:mnist_emnist_digits}): MNIST $\to$ EMNIST achieves 97.5\% and the reverse achieves 97.9\%.
This confirms that regularized bilinear features \textbf{do not overfit to MNIST writers}.

\subsubsection{Domain Transfer: MNIST $\rightarrow$ USPS}

USPS images are originally $16 \times 16$ pixels collected from U.S.\ Postal Service mail, compared to MNIST's $28 \times 28$ pixels from handwritten digit samples.
We upscale USPS to $28 \times 28$ using bilinear interpolation before applying CoM normalization.
Regularization yields \textbf{+19 pp} improvement (Table~\ref{tab:usps}, Appendix~\ref{app:cross_dataset_results}): 72.3\% vs.\ 53.4\% for the baseline.

\subsubsection{Geometric Semantics: MNIST $\rightarrow$ EMNIST-Letters}

We test whether MNIST classifiers ``see'' shape by running them on visually similar letters (Table~\ref{tab:letter_digit}, Appendix~\ref{app:cross_dataset_results}).
The model's most confident prediction for each letter is \textbf{exactly} the geometrically corresponding digit: O$\to$0 (99.6\%), I$\to$1 (87.1\%), Z$\to$2 (88.8\%), S$\to$5 (87.4\%).

\subsection{Structural Analysis: Quadratic Form Similarity}

\subsubsection{The Failure of Cosine Similarity}

Direct eigenvector comparison via cosine similarity fails to distinguish geometrically similar pairs.
MNIST-0 vs EMNIST-O (similar) yields 0.358, while MNIST-0 vs EMNIST-X (dissimilar) yields 0.339---virtually identical.
The root cause: bilinear eigensystems share eigenvector directions while class information resides in eigenvalue \emph{weights}.
See Figure~\ref{fig:3way_comparison} for visualization and Appendix~\ref{app:cosine_failure} for the full cosine similarity heatmap.

\subsubsection{Quadratic Form Similarity}

We propose comparing full decision surfaces. For class interaction matrices $A$ and $B$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{sim}(A,B) = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(A \cdot B)}{\|A\|_F \|B\|_F} \in [-1, 1]
\end{equation}
This incorporates both eigenvector alignment and eigenvalue magnitudes, with signs enabling discrimination (see Appendix~\ref{app:quadratic_form} for derivation).

Figure~\ref{fig:quadratic_heatmaps} (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_dataset_results}) shows clear discrimination: (a) strong diagonal for MNIST vs.\ EMNIST-Digits confirms writer-independent circuits; (b) bright entries at shape-matched pairs (0--O, 1--I, 2--Z, 5--S) indicate mechanistically similar circuits.
Quadratic Form Similarity produces a \textbf{large gap} between similar (0.400) and dissimilar (-0.060) pairs ($p_{value} < 10^{-4}$); Figure~\ref{fig:similarity_vs_k} (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_dataset_results}) shows this gap emerges around $k=20$ eigenvectors and remains stable thereafter.

Table~\ref{tab:ranking_analysis} (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_dataset_results}) confirms specificity: 3/4 pairs rank \#1, with mean rank 1.2 vs.\ random baseline of 13.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Regularized bilinear MLPs learn \textbf{transferable geometric circuits}: the ``0'' circuit is mathematically near-identical to the ``O'' circuit across independently trained models.
This strengthens the transparency claim---eigenvector features represent genuinely reusable shapes, not dataset-specific artifacts.
