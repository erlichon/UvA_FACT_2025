\begin{abstract}
Mechanistic interpretability typically relies on post-hoc analysis of model activations.
Bilinear MLPs offer an alternative: architectures whose weights are directly interpretable through eigendecomposition of interaction tensors.
We reproduce both main experiments from Pearce et al.\ (2024): Section 4 (Vision) on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, and Section 5 (Language) discovering sentiment negation circuits via Sparse Autoencoder analysis.

\textbf{Vision results (fully reproduced):}
Weight decay reduces effective rank from 38.5 to 21.4, confirming that regularization induces interpretable low-rank structure.
Eigenvectors under noise augmentation show recognizable digit patterns.
Our ablation disentangles noise and weight decay, finding that weight decay alone achieves the lowest rank while noise primarily improves accuracy.

\textbf{Language results (partially reproduced):}
We confirm negation circuit discovery---SAE features encoding ``not + positive'' and ``not + negative'' form opposing directions (cosine similarity $-0.16$).
However, we do \emph{not} reproduce the low-rank interaction claim: the fraction of features achieving $>$0.75 correlation is significantly lower than the claimed 69\%, and the mean rank-2 correlation saturates at 0.37--0.55.
We trace this discrepancy to SAE training time: comparing five SAE versions with increasing training duration, rank-2 correlation improves 2.5$\times$ (0.15 $\to$ 0.39), consistent with the paper's acknowledgment that correlation depends on SAE quality.
We provide evidence that the publicly available SAE artifacts have not reached the convergence levels of the internal checkpoints used in the original paper, creating a reproducibility gap.

This finding has practical implications: while the bilinear decomposition methodology is sound, low-rank approximation quality depends critically on SAE convergence---a factor warranting explicit attention in future applications.

\textbf{Extension 1 (Cross-Dataset Robustness):}
Regularized bilinear MLPs learn transferable geometric circuits---MNIST-trained models achieve 97.7\% accuracy on EMNIST-Digits and recognize letters O, I, Z, S as their corresponding digits with near-perfect accuracy.
We propose Quadratic Form Similarity to quantify structural alignment where cosine similarity fails.

\textbf{Extension 2 (CP-Decomposition):}
We explore explicit low-rank structure via CP factorization, achieving 93.8\% accuracy with disentangled, atomic feature representations at effective rank 17.5.
\end{abstract}

\section{Quadratic Form Similarity Derivation}
\label{app:quadratic_form}

For a bilinear MLP, the class-$c$ logit is a quadratic form $y_c(x) = x^\top A_c x$ where $A_c = V \,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_c)\, V^\top$.

To compare decision surfaces for two classes $A$ and $B$ (possibly from different datasets), we define \textbf{Quadratic Form Similarity}:
\[
\mathrm{sim}(A,B) = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(A \cdot B)}{\|A\|_F \|B\|_F} \in [-1, 1]
\]

For a rank-$k$ truncation using top eigenmodes, this expands to:
\[
\mathrm{sim}(A,B) = \frac{\sum_{i,j} \lambda_i^A \lambda_j^B \cos^2(v_i^A, v_j^B)}{\|\lambda^A\|_2 \|\lambda^B\|_2}
\]

\textbf{Key properties solving the cosine similarity problem:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Incorporates both eigenvector alignment and eigenvalue magnitudes, unlike cosine similarity which ignores eigenvalues
    \item Eigenvalue signs matter: same-sign directions contribute positively; opposing signs cancel
    \item Measures similarity of actual quadratic computations $x^\top A x$, not just eigenvector span
\end{itemize}

This enables discrimination between geometrically similar pairs (0--O: 0.400) and dissimilar pairs (0--X: -0.060), where cosine similarity failed (0.358 vs.\ 0.339).

\section{Cosine Similarity Limitations}
\label{app:cosine_failure}

Figure~\ref{fig:cosine_heatmap} shows the absolute cosine similarity heatmap between MNIST digit classes and EMNIST letter classes.
The heatmap demonstrates that cosine similarity fails to produce a clear distinction between geometrically similar and dissimilar class pairs.

\section{Hyperparameter Details}
\label{app:hyperparams}

\textbf{Vision Experiments.}
Architecture: 1-hidden-layer bilinear MLP, $d_{\text{hidden}} = 256$.
Training: 100 epochs, batch size 2048, AdamW optimizer, lr $10^{-3}$ with cosine annealing.
Seeds: 42--46 (5 seeds per configuration).

\textbf{Language Experiments.}
Models: \texttt{fw-medium} (16L, 1024d, 335M params), \texttt{fw-small} (12L, 768d, 162M params).
SAEs: TopK sparsity ($k=30$), expansion ratios 4--8$\times$.
Analysis: 100 randomly sampled output features per model.

\section{CO2 Tracking Details}
\label{app:co2}

All experiments tracked using \texttt{codecarbon} with Netherlands grid intensity.
Hardware: NVIDIA A100 (Snellius HPC) for language experiments; Apple M4 (MPS) for vision experiments.
Total compute: 2.02 GPU-hours.
